Sensitivity Analysis

The Sensitivity Analysis tool helps you understand how robust your gradient and direction results are when calculation parameters change. It systematically varies input settings and re-runs the full triangulation pipeline hundreds of times, then summarises how much your results shift.

Why run a sensitivity analysis? Hydraulic gradient calculations depend on parameters like base heights and head uncertainty. If small changes to these settings cause large swings in the result, that result may not be reliable enough for a technical report. Sensitivity analysis quantifies this.

Analysis Modes

HeadAnalyser supports three sensitivity analysis modes, each answering a different question:

ModeQuestion it answersStatus
Parameter Sweep How much do results change when I vary calculation parameters (base heights, head uncertainty)? Available
Leave-One-Out Which measurement points have the most influence on the result? What happens if I remove each one? Coming soon
Monte Carlo Given random noise in my head measurements, how much uncertainty is in the final gradient? Coming soon

Parameter Sweep — How It Works

1. Grid Setup

You choose two settings:

  • Steps per parameter (3–9): how many values to test for each parameter. Total runs = steps³ (e.g. 5 steps = 125 runs).
  • Spread (10–100%): how far from the baseline each parameter varies. A spread of 50% means each parameter is tested from 50% below to 50% above its current value.

The tool creates a full factorial grid — every combination of the three parameter values is tested. This ensures interactions between parameters are captured, not just individual effects.

2. The Three Parameters

ParameterShort nameWhat it controls
gradient_base_height_low BH Low Lower bound of the base-height-to-side-length ratio filter. Triangles with a ratio below this are rejected as too elongated.
gradient_base_height_high BH High Upper bound of the base-height-to-side-length ratio filter.
gradient_head_uncertainty Head Unc Assumed measurement uncertainty in hydraulic head values (metres). Affects which triangles pass the uncertainty-based rejection filter.

3. What Happens During a Run

For each parameter combination, HeadAnalyser:

  1. Temporarily overrides the three parameters with the scenario values.
  2. Re-runs the full Delaunay triangulation and gradient calculation.
  3. Applies all rejection filters with the new parameters.
  4. Records the resulting average gradient, average direction, rejection ratio, and per-reason rejection counts.
Note: The point set itself never changes during a parameter sweep — only the calculation parameters are varied. The same triangles are generated each time; only the filtering changes.

Understanding the Results

Tornado Chart

The tornado chart shows which parameter has the most influence on your selected metric. Each horizontal bar represents one parameter. The bar spans from the lowest group-mean to the highest group-mean of that metric when that parameter is varied (averaging over all combinations of the other two parameters).

  • Longest bar = most influential parameter. Focus your attention here.
  • The green dashed line shows your current baseline value.
  • If all bars are short, your result is stable — good news for your report.

Parameter Response Plot

The scatter plot shows the relationship between one parameter (x-axis) and the selected metric (y-axis). Each dot is a single run. The trend line connects group means.

  • Linear trend: the metric responds proportionally to the parameter. Predictable.
  • Step/plateau: the metric is insensitive over most of the range but jumps at a threshold. Check if your baseline is near that threshold.
  • Wide scatter around trend: the other two parameters also matter — this parameter alone doesn't explain the variation.
  • The green diamond marks your baseline.

Distribution Histogram

The histogram shows how the selected metric is distributed across all runs. This gives you a sense of the spread of possible outcomes.

  • Narrow, symmetric: results are stable. Confidence is high.
  • Wide or bimodal: results are sensitive. Report the range, not just the mean.
  • The green dashed line shows your baseline value. If it sits near the centre of the distribution, your current settings are "typical". If it's at an edge, your current result may be an extreme case.

Result Envelope KPIs

The right panel shows summary statistics:

KPIWhat it means
Gradient RangeMin–max average gradient across all runs.
Direction RangeMin–max average direction across all runs.
Within ±10°How many runs produced a direction within 10° of baseline. High = direction is robust.
Within ±20%How many runs produced a gradient within 20% of baseline. High = gradient is robust.
Stability ScoreProduct of the two "within" ratios. A single number summarising overall robustness (0–100%).

Fragile Points

Points that frequently appear in triangles that "flip" between kept and rejected across runs. A high fragility score means the point sits right on the edge of a rejection boundary — small parameter changes toggle its triangles.

Tip: Fragile points are not necessarily bad data — they may just be in areas where the triangulation geometry is borderline. But they are worth investigating if you need high confidence in your results.

Exporting Results

After a run completes, you can export the raw data:

  • CSV: one row per run, with all parameter values and computed metrics. Suitable for further analysis in Excel or Python.
  • JSON: structured export including baseline, distributions, fragile points, and run-level detail.
